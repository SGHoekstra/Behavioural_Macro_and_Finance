Running Notebook 05 inference exercises

Read the full notebook online. It displays its explanations, code and exercises in reading mode. This guide explains how to run its simulations and neural training in a local Julia/Python environment, generate posterior draws and inspect how well the coefficients are recovered.

Open the notebook source

The question

In Notebook 04, you chose how strongly CANVAS firms pass demand, costs and expected inflation into their prices. Can inflation and GDP growth reveal those three coefficients instead?

Notebook 05 uses a known-truth recovery experiment. It generates a synthetic observation at chosen coefficients, then gives the estimator only the observation. The true coefficients provide a reference for interpreting the inferred distribution. All other model parameters and the pricing mechanism remain fixed.

Before running, predict which pricing channel could be hardest to distinguish from the others using just two aggregate series.

Three ways to infer the coefficients

MethodWhat it doesWhat to inspect
ABCDraw coefficients from the prior, simulate data, retain draws close to the observationDistance definition, tolerance and accepted count
NPETrain a conditional density of coefficients given simulated outputsNetwork fit, posterior shape and parameter correlations
NRE-BTrain a contrastive classifier to estimate a likelihood-to-evidence ratio, combine it with the prior and sample with MCMCNetwork fit, chain mixing and effective sample size

The executable example runs one prior-sampled round of each neural method. It uses engines that can also run sequentially; calling an engine “SNPE” or “SNRE” does not by itself add a second simulation round. The optional sequential exercise focuses new simulations on the current posterior and explains why NPE needs a proposal correction.

A sharper histogram is not automatically a better posterior. Weak identification, network error and a misspecified simulator can all affect the result.

Run in stages

After cloning the course repository, follow the installation page and run:

cd Behavioural_Macro_and_Finance/pluto_tutorial
julia setup.jl
julia -t auto -e 'using Pluto; Pluto.run()'

Open 05_likelihood_free_calibration.jl in Pluto. Opening the notebook starts in reading mode: simulation and training stages are disabled. Enabling the local environment may install Python, PyTorch and sbi on first use.

  1. Enable the local environment. Read the observation protocol and small-budget settings.
  2. Generate the synthetic observation. Twelve quarterly inflation rates and twelve growth rates form a 24-value vector.
  3. Generate the ABC bank, optionally. Move its tolerance slider to reuse the stored distances without new ABM runs.
  4. Generate the neural training bank. Inspect the coefficient/output matrix dimensions and any failed simulations.
  5. Train NPE. Interpret all three marginals and inspect their joint relationships.
  6. Train NRE-B, optionally. It reuses the neural bank and samples using MCMC.

The default experiment uses four simulated paths per output, 100 ABC parameter draws and 200 neural training draws. NPE draws 1,000 approximate posterior samples; the optional NRE run draws 200 MCMC samples. These are teaching budgets, not an accuracy guarantee. Runtime varies by computer; start with the defaults before increasing the cost.

Match the observation to the simulator

The synthetic observation and every training output use the same horizon, units, four-path averaging and data transformations, with distinct seeds. Coefficient draws use a separate random-number generator from the economy simulations.

This averaged observation is an idealised experiment. An empirical application must specify how data from one economy, measurement errors and transformations enter the observation model. Changing the observation protocol requires changing training too. Failed or non-finite simulations block neural fitting rather than being silently removed from the estimation sample.

Recovery, diagnostics and validation

  • Recovery asks whether inference locates chosen coefficients in data from the same simulator.
  • Inference calibration checks posterior probabilities across repeated held-out synthetic experiments.
  • Posterior prediction asks whether posterior draws reproduce observed or additional features.
  • Model and policy validation require independent data, alternative mechanisms or intervention responses.

A same-model recovery exercise cannot establish that the model represents the real economy or predicts a policy intervention reliably. Even good prediction of the two fitted series can leave different pricing mechanisms observationally similar.

Exercises to start with

The full notebook gives six exercises with hints. Begin with the two that need no new economy simulations: vary the ABC tolerance and plot cost-push versus expectation coefficients from the NPE draws. Then compare a coarser data representation, generate a small posterior-predictive sample, discuss a misspecification test, or attempt a second sequential round.

For background, see Dyer et al. (2024), Black-Box Bayesian Inference for Agent-Based Models, and the official sbi Bayesian workflow and sequential-inference guide.
